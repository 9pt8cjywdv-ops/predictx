# Simulation results

Generated by `python -m genesis.simulate`. **All figures are virtual USDC produced by an assumption-driven
simulation model** (see `genesis/simworld.py`): zero-drift crypto prices, low and highly uneven niche demand
with a cold start, realistic fees and model-usage costs. They demonstrate that the ecosystem's mechanics work;
they are **not** a forecast of real-world revenue.

Each run: one genesis agent with $50, the deterministic heuristic brain (Claude usage metered at Haiku 5.5
rates), administrator wallet configured, default conservative policy (incl. $100 auto-payout limit and
$5,000 max aggregate capital). Larger payouts wait for administrator approval, which nobody gives in these
unattended runs - so 'paid to owner' understates what an attentive administrator would receive.

## Summary

- Runs: 11; ecosystems with positive realized profit: 11; extinct ecosystems: 0; ecosystems that reproduced at least once: 1.
- Total paid to the owner across runs: $1,711.62 against $550.00 of virtual capital invested.
- Every run's ledger balanced and reconciled against the simulated chain: yes.

Interpretation (read this before the numbers):

- **Individual experiments mostly fail; ecosystems did not.** Agents typically abandoned two or three
  zero-revenue niches before finding one that sells. Every unattended ecosystem ended profitable because, in
  this model, trying a new niche costs only a few dollars and every strategy has at least one viable niche.
  That is a property of the *model's assumptions*, not evidence that real agents will be profitable. Real
  markets add customer-acquisition friction, platform rules, competition and trust that the model omits.
- **Spot trading lost money**, as designed (zero-drift prices plus fees). Runs that started with spot trading
  (seeds 5, 6, 10) made their profit only after the agent switched to a service strategy.
- **Reproduction is rare.** Reaching $500 of unencumbered capital while paying out 50% of profit weekly took
  a favourable niche (seed 42: first birth in month ~5, three generations, then births stopped at the
  $5,000 aggregate-capital cap, as designed).
- **Agent death** did not occur in these runs; it is demonstrated by Test F (a never-profitable agent).
- **Unapproved payouts:** in the seed-42 run a payout exceeded the $100 auto-limit and waited for approval
  that never came, so later profit was deferred (not lost); operator costs were still reimbursed weekly.
- The number that matters most: every run's books balanced and reconciled exactly.
